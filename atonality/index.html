Atonality and Beyond

Schoenberg, twelve-tone rows, chance music, and sound as texture.

Before we start

Key takeaways

  • Atonality means no tonal center. Schoenberg made it systematic in the early 1900s.
  • Twelve-tone technique uses all 12 notes in a fixed row, so no note gets to be “home.”
  • Schoenberg is respected more than loved; his students Berg and Webern are performed more.
  • Chance music (Cage) lets coin flips or dice decide the content: let sound be sound.
  • Texture music (Ligeti) swaps melody for shifting density, speed, brightness, and mass.
  • Once the ear stops expecting resolution, dissonance reads as color, not error.
Atonality

What if there's no home note?

  • Atonality is music with no tonal center.
  • Schoenberg developed a systematic approach in the early 1900s.
  • Why: he felt the major-minor system had been exhausted by Wagner and the late Romantics.

Twelve-tone technique

All twelve notes, in a fixed row

123456789101112
  • All 12 chromatic notes are arranged in a fixed order: the row.
  • No note repeats until the entire set has been used.
  • Result: no note gets to be the privileged “home” note.

Positions 1–12 of a row; each holds a different chromatic note.

Aleatoric music

Let chance decide

  • John Cage and others used coin flips, dice, or other chance procedures to pick pitches and rhythms.
  • The structure (time signature, duration) can stay fixed.
  • The content inside it is left undetermined.
Cage's stance

Motifs and pattern

Atonal doesn't mean patternless

  • Schoenberg's rows themselves work as motifs.
  • They can be developed and returned to, like any theme.
  • What atonality removes is the idea that any note is “home.”
Motifs and pattern

A center can creep back in

Once a motif repeats enough for the ear to treat it as a reference point, some sense of center can reappear.

Textural music

No melody line to follow

  • Example: Ligeti's Atmosphères, or his micropolyphony.
  • There's no single melody line to track.
  • Listening becomes about texture rather than following a story.

A personal listening note

Dissonance as color

  • Some people come to enjoy atonal or textural music.
  • It tends to click once the ear stops expecting resolution.
  • Then dissonance reads as color rather than error.
A personal listening note

How random is “random”?

  • Random piano improvisation that produces recurring motifs is still filtered through absorbed patterns.
  • Once someone knows the rules, pure randomness is rare.
In short

Atonality is a deliberate refusal of tonic pull, while chance and texture open still other ways of organizing sound.
