Lineages: Who Learned from Whom

From Bach's sons to Beethoven's students, and the handoffs that carried the line forward.

Before we start

Key takeaways

  • Beethoven taught Czerny and Ries; Czerny taught Liszt, and Liszt's reach extends to Debussy, Ravel, and Bartók.
  • Beethoven came to Vienna at 16 hoping to study with Mozart, but studied his works for life instead.
  • Vienna around Beethoven: Haydn, Salieri, Hummel, Schubert, Weber.
  • Before Mozart: Haydn, the Mannheim school, and C.P.E. Bach as the bridge from Bach.
  • Bach's public revival came with Mendelssohn's 1829 St. Matthew Passion in Berlin.
  • Frank Bridge → Britten shows the same pattern: the student outshines the teacher.

Beethoven's line

Liszt passes it on

  • Liszt taught or influenced Debussy, Scriabin, and Busoni.
  • Through masterclasses, he reached Ravel and Bartók.
  • Further links (Taneyev–Tchaikovsky–Rubinstein) carry the line into the 20th century.
  • Within a few handoffs, the influence reaches Debussy, Ravel, Bartók, and Prokofiev.

Beethoven and Mozart

The lesson that never happened

  • Beethoven traveled to Vienna at 16 hoping to study with Mozart.
  • Mozart died before it could happen.
  • Mozart reportedly heard the young Beethoven improvise and urged people to watch him.

The Bach bridge

C.P.E. Bach

Bach was already dead. His sons were the bridge, especially C.P.E., whose keyboard works Mozart studied as a child.

Bach

Bach in his own time

  • In his lifetime, a working church musician in Leipzig.
  • At his death in 1750, his music was widely considered old-fashioned.
  • Mozart and Beethoven studied him privately.

A parallel pattern

Frank Bridge and Benjamin Britten

  • Bridge taught Britten privately, from ages 10 to 14.
  • You can clearly hear Bridge in early Britten: harmonic restlessness, sea imagery, and English melancholy.
